## The situation

Many of our partners wanted to offer flights to their own customers without building a travel business of their own. Our answer was OTaaS, Online Travel as a Service, which let a partner plug into our booking infrastructure through a co-branded landing page, a dedicated micro-site, an embedded iframe or a fully branded white label.

Each partnership depended on someone who could turn a commercial agreement into a working technical setup, and over time that responsibility became mine.

## My role

I took ownership of OTaaS in 2023, while I was the CMS Specialist. It remained part of my remit as Technical Product Coordinator and into my role as Associate Product Manager. Whenever a new partnership was agreed, I led the technical side from scoping through to launch and support.

## The lens I brought

I was the technical lead for the Nigerian team. When a partner described what they wanted, I could see what it would mean for the product, for engineering, and for the operations team who would support it after launch.

## Decisions I made

- **I matched each partner to the right integration.** After the partner meetings, I determined which option suited the agreement, whether a landing page, a micro-site, an iframe or a white label, and wrote the brief and product specification.
- **I configured each partnership myself.** In our back-office system, I created each partner's affiliate and tracking IDs, set up UTM tracking, and applied the discounts the partnership called for.
- **I owned delivery from start to finish.** I gathered brand guidelines, coordinated the front-end and back-end teams, tested every setup and ran UAT. After launch, I connected our team with the partner's team for ongoing support.
- **I worked across a wide range of partners.** A few that stand out are MTN, whose landing page on the MTN marketplace app became a long-standing route for customers to find us, and Zenith Bank; both were among our top revenue partners. For a global partnership with Mastercard, I managed the Nigerian implementation of a dedicated micro-site. Uber and Samsung were also among our partners.
- **I led a two-way partnership end to end.** With GIGM, we provided flights and they provided buses. I set up their flights white label from start to finish, then became the product owner for buses, an entirely new product for us. I wrote the proposition and the process documentation, trained the operations team and supported them day to day, and built a Looker Studio dashboard that I used to report on buses in management meetings.

## Payments

Payments ran through every partnership. Some partners, such as Zenith Bank, required that only their own payment method be available on their white label, so payment rules became a standard part of each specification I wrote. Engineering made the configuration changes; I defined what each partner required.

Beyond partners, I was the technical owner for Paystack, our main payment provider, across B2C, B2B and B2B2C. When a payment failed or the service went down, I reviewed the logs to establish whether the problem sat with our integration or with the provider, then escalated to engineering with clear findings.

When Quickteller moved its payment service to a new version of its API, I studied the integration documentation, took part in the technical conversations with their team, and wrote the requirements for our re-integration. The work was ready and awaiting engineering capacity.

## What happened

Partners went live through every route we offered, from landing pages to a dedicated micro-site and white labels. Buses gave us a new revenue stream alongside flights.

## What I learned

**Being the technical lead is a product role.** It was never written into my job description, yet anything technical came to me first. Doing it well meant understanding the system, the partner and the team deeply enough to move each one forward.

**I learned payments by doing the work.** Reading integration documentation, tracing failed payments and writing requirements taught me a great deal. Next year, I am formalising that experience with a payments product management course.
