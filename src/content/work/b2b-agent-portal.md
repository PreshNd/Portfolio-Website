## The situation

The portal is where B2B travel agents book with us. When I joined, it was already being built. The phases were planned and development had started, but it had stalled.

The problem wasn't the code. Feedback and QA findings weren't reaching the developers in a form they could act on. The Nigerian business team and the South African developers were talking past each other.

## My role

I was the Product Owner. I picked it up mid-build, took it to launch in 2023, and owned it from then on: new features, bug fixes and adoption.

## The lens I brought

I could speak both languages. I understood what the agents and the team managing them needed, and I could turn that into something developers could build and test.

## Decisions I made

- **I became the translator.** Business feedback and QA findings came through me and went to the developers as clear, testable issues.
- **I drove UAT to launch.** I tested the portal against what the business needed and got it over the line.
- **I became the go-to person after launch.** When the team managing the agents hit an error, they came to me. I understood the system and could explain it.
- **I kept fixes moving on shared resources.** The portal was built for Nigeria first. Other markets and other parts of the group then took copies and adapted them, all sharing the same developers. Getting our own changes prioritised was hard, so I set up a quarterly rhythm of fixes and stayed on top of it.

## What happened

The portal launched in 2023 and powers about 10% of daily bookings. Other markets later used it as the starting point for their own versions.

## What I learned

**Launch is the start of ownership, not the end.** After go-live, I became the person everyone came to with problems. Those conversations are where my best roadmap ideas came from, including bringing CRM into the portal.

**Shared products need a steady voice.** When one product serves many markets, a feature that's done for one team can look done for everyone. Getting our needs built meant making the case again and again, and keeping a steady rhythm of fixes.
