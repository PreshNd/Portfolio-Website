## The situation

The portal is where B2B travel agents book with us. When I joined, it was already being built: the phases had been planned and development had begun, but progress had stalled.

The problem was not the code. Feedback and QA findings were not reaching the developers in a form they could act on, and the Nigerian business team and the South African developers were, in effect, speaking different languages.

## My role

I was the Product Owner. I picked the portal up mid-build, took it to launch in 2023, and owned it from then on, including new features, bug fixes and adoption.

## The lens I brought

I could speak both languages. I understood what the agents and the team managing them needed, and I could turn those needs into work that developers could build and test.

## Decisions I made

- **I became the bridge.** All business feedback and QA findings came through me and went to the developers as clear, testable issues.
- **I drove UAT through to launch.** I tested the portal against what the business needed and saw it over the line.
- **I became the point of contact after launch.** When the team managing the agents hit a problem, they came to me, because I understood the system and could explain what was happening.
- **I kept improvements moving on shared resources.** The portal was built for Nigeria first. Other markets and other parts of the group later adapted their own versions, all relying on the same developers. Getting our changes prioritised was difficult, so I established a quarterly cycle of fixes and stayed on top of it.

## What happened

The portal launched in 2023 and now powers about 10% of daily bookings. Other markets went on to use it as the starting point for their own versions.

## What I learned

**Launch is the beginning of ownership, not the end.** After go-live, I became the person everyone brought their problems to. Those conversations produced my best roadmap ideas, including bringing CRM into the portal.

**A shared product needs a steady voice.** When one product serves many markets, a feature that is finished for one team can look finished for everyone. Getting our needs built meant making the case again and again, supported by a reliable rhythm of fixes.
