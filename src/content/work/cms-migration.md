## The situation

The company ran its websites on an internal CMS built long before I joined. I inherited it, and I used it every day. Over time it fell behind. It no longer fit the languages and tools modern websites are built with.

About 11 markets ran on it, and three of them had more than 8,000 pages each. The plan was a company-wide move to a new CMS, starting with the priority markets: Nigeria, South Africa and the Global site.

## My role

I was the Product Owner. I started the migration as Technical Product Coordinator and carried it on as Associate Product Manager.

A lot of people were in the room. The Head of Marketing as project owner. The marketing and product teams. Back-end, front-end and QA engineers. And two external contractors, one for WordPress and one for SEO. My job was to keep all of them moving toward the same pages.

## The lens I brought

I was both the old system's daily operator and the new system's owner. I knew where every feature lived, what editors relied on, and what would quietly break if we forgot it.

## Decisions I made

- **I wrote the map.** I documented the old CMS: its features, functions and schema. That document became the guide for the whole migration.
- **I shaped the new system.** I was a key decision-maker on the new CMS architecture and on the page UI and UX.
- **I led UAT.** In phase one, I tested every batch the engineers migrated and signed it off against the documentation.
- **I kept it moving when the plan changed.** Phase one ran on a headless CMS built by our Python team, with a separate front end planned on top. When that team was needed on other business priorities, the strategy changed to WordPress Multisite. We had no WordPress developers in-house, so an external contractor built the custom themes. I carried the documentation, testing and sign-off across to the new setup.

## What happened

Key pages are migrated and live. Phase two was completed, and phase three was underway. The migration is not fully complete: the remaining pages are moving across in phases.

## What I'd do differently

**Start with the problem, not the platform.** The platform question came up before the problem was fully on the table. If I started again, I'd lay it out first: how the old system really works, who lives with it every day, what they need from it long term, and how much time and how many people we have. Then I'd choose the platform, or the mix of tools, that fits.

The final call wasn't mine at the time. I'd still speak up earlier and louder, with the old system's documentation in hand.
