## The situation

The company's websites ran on an internal CMS that had been built long before I joined. I inherited it and worked in it every day, so I saw first-hand how far it had fallen behind. It no longer fitted the languages and tools that modern websites are built with.

Around 11 markets depended on it, and three of them carried more than 8,000 pages each. The decision was made to move the whole company to a new CMS, beginning with the priority markets of Nigeria, South Africa and the Global site.

## My role

I was the Product Owner. I began the migration as Technical Product Coordinator and continued to lead it as Associate Product Manager.

The project brought many people together. The Head of Marketing was the project owner, working alongside the marketing and product teams, back-end, front-end and QA engineers, and two external contractors, one for WordPress and one for SEO. My responsibility was to keep everyone working towards the same outcome, page by page.

## The lens I brought

I was both the daily operator of the old system and the owner of the new one. I knew where every feature lived, what the editors relied on, and what would quietly break if it were overlooked.

## Decisions I made

- **I documented the old system in full.** I mapped the legacy CMS's features, functions and data schema, and that document became the reference for the entire migration.
- **I helped shape the new system.** I was a key decision-maker on the new CMS architecture and on the page UI and UX.
- **I led UAT.** In phase one, I tested every batch the engineers migrated and signed each one off against the documentation.
- **I kept the work moving when the strategy changed.** Phase one was built on a headless CMS by our Python team, with a separate front end planned on top. When that team was needed for other business priorities, the strategy moved to WordPress Multisite. With no WordPress developers in-house, an external contractor built the custom themes, and I carried the documentation, testing and sign-off across to the new setup.

## What happened

Key pages have been migrated and are live. Phase two was completed and phase three was underway, with the remaining pages moving across in phases.

## What I'd do differently

**I would start with the problem, not the platform.** The platform question arose before the problem had been fully set out. If I began again, I would first establish how the old system really worked, who lived with it every day, what they needed from it in the long term, and how much time and capacity we had. Only then would I choose the platform, or the combination of tools, that fitted.

The final decision was not mine to make at the time. Even so, I would raise these questions earlier and more firmly, with the old system's documentation in hand.
